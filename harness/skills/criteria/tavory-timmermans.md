# Fit, plausibility and relevance: Tavory & Timmermans (2014)

Phase 2 rates every puzzle on three criteria from *Abductive Analysis: Theorizing
Qualitative Research* (Tavory & Timmermans 2014). Each passage in quotation marks
below is copied from the book, which is in this repository at
`docs/abduction.md`; a test checks every one against that file, so this rubric
cannot drift from the book.

The materials are Phase 1's. Phase 2 interprets; it runs nothing and counts
nothing, so a reviewer judges a puzzle against the pattern card and the evidence
behind it, never against a new measurement.

## Fit

> "The fit of a claim refers to the extent to which it follows from the presented evidence and at the same time transcends the immediacy of the observations."
> "An empirical restatement of the data would have a perfect fit but add little analytical purchase."
> "Are the theoretical claims supported by the empirical materials?"

Judge a puzzle's fit against Phase 1's evidence — the pattern card's Result,
Verdict, Robustness, Worst fits and Deviations — because Phase 2 runs no new
analysis and the claim has to follow from that evidence and transcend it. A
puzzle that restates the pattern is not a puzzle, and one that is silent about
the negative cases and thin cells its card records does not fit either; where a
claim needs a check, the reviewer writes it down as a question for Phase 3.

## Plausibility

> "Are the theoretical claims stronger than competing theories?"
> "Even if the inference does seem to fit the observations, there could be alternative theoretical accounts that make equal, if not better, sense to the audiences we speak and write to."

Rate the puzzle against the strongest rival account the literature can offer,
and search for that rival adversarially, citing what you find. A puzzle that an
existing theory already explains is a breakdown, not a puzzle, and the review
says so plainly.

## Relevance

> "Do the theorizations matter in the broader intellectual community?"
> "If the theorization the researcher constructs is to be the hook for relevance, the question “What is this a case of?” transforms into an inquiry about whether these cases matter in a broader theoretical debate."
> "As opposed to questions of fit and plausibility, there are no clear methodological guidelines researchers can follow in the search for theoretical relevance."

Name the debate the puzzle enters and what a resolution would give researchers
who are not studying this corpus. Relevance is a warrant the reviewer argues for
and cites; the book offers no method for it, so the argument has to carry itself.
